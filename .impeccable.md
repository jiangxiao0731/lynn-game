## Design Context

### Users

This is a portfolio demo, not a commercial release. Its primary audience is portfolio reviewers watching a recorded showcase rather than players discovering it through a store. The demo should quickly communicate strong Godot/C# engineering, coherent art direction, environmental storytelling, interaction design, and a polished beginning-to-end gameplay arc.

The player's job is to explore a polluted seabed, understand how pollution has harmed its inhabitants, gather lost shimmer memories, and restore the ecosystem through purification rather than violence.

### Brand Personality

Gentle, melancholic, hopeful. The game treats polluted creatures as victims rather than enemies and frames every action around protection, care, recovery, and returning life to the ocean. It should feel authored and emotionally sincere, never like a generic neon action game.

### Aesthetic Direction

A cohesive hand-painted underwater storybook built from the existing painterly assets. Use layered parallax, sediment, drifting life, restrained bioluminescence, and visible polluted-to-restored environmental transitions. Cyan and teal remain part of the ocean palette, but glowing dark glass must not dominate every interface.

Do not generate new assets. Do not mix the separate pixel-art libraries into the painterly runtime merely to increase asset count. Reuse as many stylistically compatible existing images as possible, with procedural graphics limited to motion, guidance, feedback, and environmental layering.

The presentation is capture-first: framing, contrast, pacing, animation readability, and clear story beats must survive video compression and remain legible at a glance.

### Quality Bar

Flagship portfolio demo. The complete playable path should be long enough to demonstrate exploration, dialogue, collection, transformation, purification, environmental restoration, and resolution without filler. Every scene shown in the final recording must be intentional, readable, cohesive, and free of warnings or visibly broken animation.

### Design Principles

1. **Protection is the verb.** Use “净化、修复、倾听、唤醒、守护,” never combat-first framing or kill language.
2. **Let restoration be visible.** Player progress must change the environment, creatures, color, motion, and sound—not only counters and dialogue.
3. **One painterly world.** Prefer the existing hand-painted character, zone, pollution, memory, note, and background assets; exclude stylistically conflicting pixel art.
4. **Design for the camera.** Keep the player and objective readable, compose each zone as a showcase shot, and avoid HUD clutter or dead traversal.
5. **Motion explains state.** Fix sprite slicing, pivots, scale, loops, easing, and feedback; use calm organic movement and clear purification transitions instead of bounce-heavy decoration.
6. **Depth without filler.** Extend the demo through varied objectives, optional discoveries, environmental transformation, and staged boss purification rather than repetitive shard grinding.
7. **No paid or new generation.** Improve through selection, editing, compositing, animation, shaders, particles, and reuse of existing assets only.
