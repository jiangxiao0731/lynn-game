# Narrative and learning architecture

## Purpose and voice

The story is written for a portfolio reviewer and a general teenage-to-adult player.
The language is direct, emotionally sincere, and easy to read during play. It avoids
technical lectures, childish jokes, combat language, and vague environmental slogans.

Every chapter follows the same causal structure:

1. **Notice the harm** through a resident or a visible environmental change.
2. **Trace the source** from the damaged habitat back to a human system.
3. **Restore a process** through play: remove a blockage, return oxygen, or control heat.
4. **Name the limit** of the repair: cleanup helps, but prevention must happen upstream.

## Three-chapter knowledge spine

| Chapter | Environmental question | Knowledge carried by the story | Player verb |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tidepool Nursery | How does waste reach marine habitats? | Much marine pollution begins on land. Plastic can be eaten or trap wildlife; abandoned fishing gear can keep catching animals; oil harms bodies, eggs, and habitat. | Listen, examine, collect, release |
| Frostbound Trench | Why can polluted water lose its life? | Runoff can carry excess nitrogen and phosphorus. These nutrients feed algal blooms; decay uses oxygen and can create low-oxygen water. Moving water helps oxygen reach a nursery, but the source still needs to be controlled. | Trace, reconnect, restore flow |
| The Silent Lighthouse | Why is a hotter ocean dangerous? | Greenhouse gases trap heat, and the ocean absorbs most of the excess. Long heat stress can make coral bleach. Cleaner energy and lower emissions reduce pressure on reefs, while recovery still takes time. | Stabilize, redirect, protect |

## Information layers

- **Environment:** shows damage before any explanation.
- **Resident dialogue:** gives the impact a personal voice.
- **Objective:** turns one cause into one clear action.
- **Restoration node:** delivers one short fact at the exact moment the player changes the system.
- **Memory or fragment:** preserves evidence and rewards optional attention.
- **Guardian:** represents an ecosystem response, not an evil enemy.
- **Chapter result:** connects local repair to prevention and long-term responsibility.

No single layer carries the whole lesson. A player who follows only the main path still
understands the causal chain; optional memories add emotional and historical depth.

## Terminology

- Use **restore**, **release**, **guide**, **reconnect**, and **protect**.
- Use **polluted creature** or **guardian**, not enemy or monster in player-facing text.
- Use **source**, **runoff**, **algal bloom**, **oxygen**, **ocean heat**, and **coral bleaching** only after their meaning is shown in context.
- Use **Shimmer**, **Tidal Shard**, **Tidal Memory**, and **Tidal Key** consistently.
